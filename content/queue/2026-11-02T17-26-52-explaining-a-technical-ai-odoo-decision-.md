Explaining AI decisions to non-technical stakeholders often reveals gaps.

Recently, a client approached me about automating their invoice processing with an AI agent. They envisioned a seamless, hands-free solution that would cut down on manual entry. While I appreciate the enthusiasm, I had to pause. The underlying data wasn’t ready. We were facing dirty master data and inconsistent formats, which would compromise the AI's effectiveness. I explained that deploying a sophisticated AI tool won’t resolve foundational data issues. It would only amplify them.
Our conversation shifted from the excitement of automation to the gritty reality of data hygiene. We outlined a phased approach: cleansing their data first, then evaluating the right AI approach. This decision wasn't easy, but it was necessary. The goal is always to ensure that what we deploy works, even on Monday morning.
Have you faced pushback when explaining AI decisions to operations teams?
