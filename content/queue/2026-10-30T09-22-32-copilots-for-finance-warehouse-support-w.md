Recent discussions about LLMs often spotlight the promise of AI copilots in various sectors. While the idea of automating parts of finance and warehouse operations sounds appealing, I've learned to be cautious. Copilots must enhance human expertise, not replace it.

In my experience, attempting to automate complex support tasks can lead to more complications than it solves. A finance team may trust a copilot to draft a report, but without a keen human eye, essential context could be missed. Misinterpretations can easily propagate through operations when decisions are based on AI outputs without oversight.

We regularly deal with clients who want to streamline processes. However, I've firmly pushed back against automating functions like vendor communications or financial reconciliations. The nuances in these tasks demand human judgment. If an AI copilot can't do more than fill in blanks, it shouldn't be in the driver's seat.

A human touch in operations isn’t just a preference; it’s necessary for accuracy and trust. What’s your experience with balancing automation and human oversight?
