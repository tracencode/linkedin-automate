In manufacturing, the balance between customization and standardization can make or break an implementation. I remember a project where a client insisted on tailoring every aspect of their production module. They wanted unique workflows, custom reports, and specific alerts for each machine. It seemed reasonable at first. But as we dove deeper, it became clear that this level of customization would lead to complexity, especially with Odoo’s updates and maintenance.

We pushed back, advocating for a more standardized approach with minor tweaks that would still meet their operational needs. The argument was simple: a complex setup means higher risks during upgrades and a steeper learning curve for users. Their team needed to focus on production efficiency, not wrestling with a labyrinthine system.

In the end, we agreed on a middle ground—customizations that added value without losing sight of usability. Now, the system is robust and flexible enough to adapt as their operations evolve without losing stability.

Have you faced a similar dilemma between customization and standardization in your implementations?
