The latest tools are blurring lines between drafting and deployment.

With the likes of Allos and EZClaw making waves, I understand the allure of AI that can write into production data. But I've seen it backfire.
In one instance, a client wanted to use an AI agent to manage customer support tickets. They envisioned a system that would draft responses automatically, reducing the load on their team. But the truth is, drafting is a far cry from sending. The nuances of customer interactions require human judgment. An AI may draft perfectly engaging responses, but once it gets into a production environment, the stakes rise.
Errors or miscommunications can harm customer relations. This is where many teams stumble. They overlook the fact that allowing AI to directly modify production data without strict oversight can lead to mistakes. We must ensure robust validation processes are in place before anything goes live. It’s not just about AI capabilities, but also about the human touch that finalizes the message.
How do you balance between drafting benefits and the risks of production data?
