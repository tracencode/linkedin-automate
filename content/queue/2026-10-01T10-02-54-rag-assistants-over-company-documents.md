RAG assistants can be helpful, but they can also hallucinate. When using them over company documents, the value often depends on the quality of the underlying data. Recently, we had a case where a client used an AI assistant to pull insights from their product documentation. The assistant generated some useful summaries but also presented incorrect details that led to confusion in a meeting.

The challenge was that the documents had inconsistencies, and the assistant filled in gaps with assumptions. This is where RAG can mislead teams that rely on it without validating the output.

In contrast, when the same assistant is trained on clean, structured data, it can lead to actionable insights. It’s about understanding the boundaries of AI assistance and knowing when to validate or question the output. We need to remind ourselves that AI is a tool, not a replacement for critical thinking and domain knowledge. How do you keep your data clean and ensure your AI outputs remain reliable?
