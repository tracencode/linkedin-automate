The latest open-source AI agents promise rapid deployment.

Tools like Allos and EZClaw might seem appealing, but they often lead teams to automate processes that should remain manual. I've witnessed this firsthand. A client rushed to implement an automated ticketing system using a shiny new agent. Within days, they faced chaos. Their unique workflows, riddled with exceptions, broke under the weight of automation. Instead of improving efficiency, the system introduced delays and errors.
At TraceNcode, we’ve learned that not every task needs automation. Sometimes, a checklist suffices. While these new tools are designed for ease, they can’t grasp the nuances of every operation. Understanding when to automate is crucial. Blindly integrating new AI agents without a solid grasp of existing processes can derail operations.
Have you seen situations where automation caused more problems than it solved?
